{#s053-h .heading} [Eighteenth distinction.]

{#s053-01} On the eighteenth distinction it is asked whether the whole pre-existing charity is corrupted, so that no reality numerically the same remains in the greater and the lesser charity. It seems so: because if it were not corrupted, then the form of charity would be changed from less to more; this is false, since it is a simple and invariable form. To the contrary, *On Generation* 1: what is increased must remain, etc.

{#s053-02} He sets down the opinion of Godfrey, *Quodlibet* 11, question 3, that nothing of the pre-existing charity remains in the increased charity. And afterwards, where he says, ‘This opinion is confirmed, that as in several species,’ etc.: this too is Godfrey, *Quodlibet* 7, question 7. And afterwards, where he says, ‘It is confirmed secondly, because in like manner more and less seem to be posited,’ etc.: this is likewise Godfrey, *Quodlibet* 9, question 4. Afterwards the Doctor argues against him in six ways. In the sixth way, where the Doctor says, ‘it is proved according to this position by conceding the conclusion,’ etc.: this is Godfrey, *Quodlibet* 11, question 3. And afterwards, where he says, ‘to these arguments it is replied: to the first,’ etc.: this is Godfrey in the same place.

{#s053-03} But coming to the question, it is said that when the second degree supervenes the first is not corrupted: for otherwise, if it were corrupted, it would be generation and not augmentation, against Aristotle, *On Generation* 1. But for fuller clarification four things will briefly be seen. First, supposing that charity is increased, whether this is by degrees. Second, I shall speak to the point. Third, supposing that those degrees are not corrupted, whether they are distinct or the same in reality. Fourth, how these degrees make one.

{#s053-04} As to the first article, the holy Doctor says that augmentation does not come about by degrees. But the Doctor rejects this and says that it does. And it is argued in this way. It is impossible to conceive that from the least perfect a more perfect comes to be except by degrees: for otherwise, that there should be more and less and there should not be degrees is a contradiction in terms. But charity becomes greater from lesser, as is held by faith; therefore etc. But note here that degrees are twofold, namely individual and essential. I do not mean that augmentation comes about by essential degrees, but by individual ones.

{#s053-05} [[p. 86|v° · Di.xviii. Q.ii]]As to the second, it is the opinion of Thomas and of Godfrey, where cited above, that when the second degree supervenes the first is corrupted. The Doctor rejects this and says that it is not so: for augmentation is an addition to a pre-existing quantity; therefore, if it is added to a pre-existing quantity, it seems that the first degree is not corrupted. And Aristotle thinks this in defining augmentation. Again, it would follow that in the rarefaction of the form of the species in the Eucharist, upon consecration (which a greater or lesser quantity necessarily follows), the preceding quantity would be corrupted, which is manifestly against the faith: since those accidents, numerically the same, that were in the bread and wine remain once the consecration is made. But if anyone says that the question is about charity, I say that what is said of charity will be said of accidental forms, and conversely, in the matter at hand.

{#s053-06} As to the third principal [article], it is said that the degrees are really distinct, which is clear: for when one degree remains another is destroyed, and so it seems that they are really distinguished. As to the fourth, it is said that they make one by a unity of homogeneity, as many waters make one water, since they are of the same nature: so these degrees. But [it is objected] if one degree is given to a whiteness or a charity perfect in the tenth degree, it is an accident, since it comes after complete being. I say no: because what comes after complete being is an accident if it is of another genus than the first; but degree and degree are not distinguished in genus; consequently what supervenes is not an accident either.

{#s053-07} To the argument at the beginning, I say that although the form is invariable in such a way that it cannot pass beyond its latitude, yet within that latitude it has degrees according to which it can vary.
