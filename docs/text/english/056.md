{#s056-h .heading} [Nineteenth distinction.]

{#s056-01} On the nineteenth distinction it is asked whether the divine persons are equal in greatness. [[p. 90|v° · D.i.xix. Q.i]]It seems not, since according to Hilary the Father is greater than the Son. To the contrary, in the Creed of Athanasius: ‘in this Trinity nothing is greater or less.’ To which it is said that they are: for the Church confesses it, saying ‘the divine persons are co-eternal and co-equal with one another.’

{#s056-02} But in this question four things will be seen. First, what is the foundation of equality, both proximate and remote. Second, whether the divine persons are equal. Third, whether equality signifies a positive relation. Fourth, whether equality is multiplied in divine things.

{#s056-03} As to the first, it is said that the Philosopher, *Metaphysics* 5, divides relation into relations of identity, of likeness, and of equality. And he says that those things are the same whose substance is one, while those are alike whose quality is one. And so we shall say that the remote foundation of identity is substance, while the proximate foundation is the unity of substance; the remote foundation of likeness is quality, and the proximate is the unity of that quality. Likewise the remote foundation of equality is quantity, but the proximate is the unity of that quantity. But it must be noted that quantity is twofold, namely quantity of mass and quantity of power. So the Philosopher holds, *On Generation* 2, chapter 1; and Augustine, *On the Trinity* 5, says that in things which are not great by mass, to be better is the same as to be greater. Hence, although quantity of mass is not posited in God, quantity of power is nevertheless posited in God, as the Philosopher proves near the end of *Physics* 8.

{#s056-04} As to the second, it is said that the divine persons are equal in greatness of power. This is clear: for the greatness of power is not taken by comparison with the notional acts, or acts *ad intra*, but by comparison with what is *ad extra*; but every power *ad extra* that one person has, the other has too; therefore etc.

{#s056-05} As to the third article, it is said that equality as a word imports a privation, since, as is clear from *Metaphysics* 4, the equal is opposed privatively to the great and the small. But equality in reality imports a positive relation, or position: since that relation is positive which imports, or which belongs to God by reason of, a positive foundation; but equality is such — the minor is clear. For the foundation of equality is the essence or an attributal perfection, and the ground of the foundation is infinity, through which it is positive.

{#s056-06} As to the fourth, it is the view of Richard that equality is not multiplied in divine things. But the Doctor holds the opposite. For when the foundations are multiplied, the relations too are [[p. 91|r° · Di.xix. Q.ii]]multiplied; but the foundations are so multiplied, since they are not wholly the same outside the intellect. This is clear: for equality is taken according to the attributal perfections, for instance according to wisdom and justice and the like; and these attributal perfections are not altogether the same outside the intellect, but rather are distinguished in some way from the nature of the thing, as is had in distinction 12. To the argument at the beginning it is said that greatness is there taken for principality.
