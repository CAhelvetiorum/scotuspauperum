{#s050-h .heading} [Sixteenth distinction.]

{#s050-01} [[p. 81|r° · Di.xvi. Q.i]]On the sixteenth distinction it is asked whether, in the visible mission of the Holy Spirit, the Holy Spirit is related to the creature. But before I come to the question I set down some points by way of conclusions. First, that the Holy Spirit ought not to have been sent visibly in the time of the written Law, is clear: for he was not to be sent except in the time of the fullness of grace, a sign of the fullness of grace being something visible. Second, that he ought to have been sent visibly at the time when the Church was first founded, is clear: for there was difficulty in believing the things that were preached at the beginning of the Church, and therefore the greatest sign had to be applied to it. Third, that he was sent under certain species with good reason: first in the species of a dove, to signify humility to those receiving him; second, he was sent in the species of a bright cloud, to illumine the truth; third, he was sent in the species of a breath, to bind together fraternal charity; fourth, he was sent in the species of a fiery tongue, on account of charity, for the preaching of perfect holiness and for the kindling of cold hearts.

{#s050-02} But coming to the question, the answer is negative; and the reason is that he would be changed, since at first he would not have the relation and afterwards would. But here there will be three things. First, the point of the question will be addressed. Second, whether God is related to the creature through an act of our reason. Third, whether certain signs in which the Holy Spirit appeared were true signs.

{#s050-03} As to the first, it is said that God is not related to the creature by a real relation, nor by a formal one: for a relation arises from the extremes, but in God it does not arise from the extreme, since then he would be changed by a new relation to the creature. But it is said that he is related by a fundamental relation, and that inequality and priority are fundamental relations. As to the second, it is said that he is not related through an act of our reason, as some say, nor through an act of the divine reason, as Henry says, but he is related through a fundamental act.

{#s050-04} As to the third, it is said that he appeared in the modes or signs mentioned above. But on this some say that it was not a true dove nor a true fire, since it did not burn. But according to the truth it is said that it was a true fire and a true dove. For the Church says: ‘The Holy Spirit was seen in the species of a dove’; and so one must say that it was a true dove created by God. Again, because if it were a sign that appeared and did not exist, it would be deceptive. But [[p. 82|v° · Di.xvii. Q.i]]the dove was not a deceptive sign but a true one; for according to Augustine the Holy Spirit shows himself infallibly. But as to how the fire was deprived of its operation, so that it did not burn, nor did the dove eat: it is said that supernaturally and immediately the fire and the dove ceased to be.
