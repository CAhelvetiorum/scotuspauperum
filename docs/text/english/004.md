{#s004-01} The second question of the Prologue, where it is asked whether the supernatural knowledge necessary to the wayfarer has been sufficiently handed down in Sacred Scripture. It seems not: for necessary knowledge was never lacking to the human race; [but] Sacred Scripture did not exist under the law of nature; and the whole of Sacred Scripture did not exist under the Mosaic law, but only the Old Testament.

{#s004-02} To the contrary is Augustine, *The City of God* XI, c. 3. And the Doctor answers in the affirmative; for in this question there are no [conflicting] opinions. But for fuller explanation it is said that this doctrine is true, and necessary, and sufficient for the wayfarer to attain his end. For Augustine, *The City of God* XI, c. 3, speaking of Sacred Scripture, says: “In it we put our faith concerning those things of which it is not expedient to be ignorant, and which we are not competent to know by ourselves.”

{#s004-03} On this question there are many heresies which corrupt or condemn Sacred Scripture, either the whole of it or a part, as is found in Augustine’s book *On Heresies*. For some [[p. 8|v°]] heretics accept nothing of Scripture, as the pagans. Some reject the Old Testament in particular, as the Manichaeans, as appears in the book *On the Usefulness of Believing*. Some say that the Old Testament comes from an evil principle, as certain heretics. Some accept only the Old Testament, as the Jews. Some accept something of each, as the Saracens. Some [reject] something said in the New, as those heretics who accept part of that Testament and disregard part, as in James 5: “Confess your sins one to another.” From this, however, they fell into error concerning the sacrament of penance, saying that it can be administered by anyone at all who is not a priest.

{#s004-04} But against these there are ten ways. The first is prophetic foretelling: for the prophets foretold many things which have been fulfilled, as Augustine witnesses, *The City of God* X, at the end. Secondly, the concord of the Scriptures: for the writers of the canonical books, variously circumstanced and living at different times, agreed entirely on matters that are not evident; Augustine treats this way in *The City of God* XVIII, c. 43. Thirdly, the authority of the writers, to whom such faith is given, as to Paul concerning the things seen in his rapture, and to the apostles and evangelists; of whom Richard says, *De Trinitate* I, c. 3: “The oracles of God have been handed down to us by men of the highest sanctity.”

{#s004-05} Fourthly, from the diligence of those who received [them]. For the writers of the sacred canon exercised the greatest diligence concerning the truth; of which Augustine speaks in *The City of God* XVIII, c. 38: how the writing of Enoch, which Jude mentions in his Epistle, is not received into the canon, nor many other writings mentioned in the Books of Kings; where he intimates that only those writings were received into the canon which their authors wrote not as men but as prophets, by divine inspiration. The same Augustine, in his first letter to Jerome, cited in the *Decretum*, dist. 9: “To those books of the Scriptures alone which are called canonical I have learned to pay this honour, that I most firmly believe that none of their authors erred in anything in writing them. Others, however, I read in such a way that, however much they excel in holiness and learning, I do not think a thing true because they thought so”, etc.

{#s004-06} Fifthly, from the reasonableness of their contents. For what is more reasonable than that God, as the last end, should be loved above all things, and one’s neighbour as oneself? “On these two commandments hangeth the whole law and the prophets” (Matthew 22); and “This do, and thou shalt live.” Sixthly, from the unreasonableness of the several errors, which is shown thus. What is more unreasonable than what the pagans allege in defence of their idolatry, worshipping the works of their own hands, than which nothing is vainer, as appears from the philosophers, since they look to gluttony and coition as happiness, as is said in *Ethics* I of King Sardanapalus? Why do the Jews condemn the New Testament [[p. 9|r° · Q.iii]] and still await the first coming of Christ?

{#s004-07} Seventhly, from the stability of the Church, according to Augustine, *On the Usefulness of Believing* 8. Eighthly, from the clarity of the miracles, as Richard has it, *De Trinitate* I, c. 2, saying: “Lord, if it is error, we have been deceived by you; for these things have been confirmed by such signs as could not have been done except by you.”

{#s004-08} Ninthly, from the testimony of outsiders, that is, of those who are outside the law: as Josephus in *Antiquities* XVIII gives a most beautiful testimony concerning Christ, where among other things written of Jesus he says: “This man was the Christ”; where he also acknowledges his true teaching and his resurrection. Likewise from the prophecy of the Sibyl, *The City of God* XVIII, c. 23. Tenthly, that God does not fail those who seek salvation with their heart; and so many seeking salvation have been converted to this law, as Paul and many others. And so by these ten ways he concludes that the doctrine of the canonical Scriptures is true.

{#s004-09} And note, in the seventh way, concerning the sect of Mahomet: how it began six hundred years after the law of Christ, and that, God willing, it will shortly come to an end, having been much weakened in the year 1300. And this the brother of the King of Armenia, ⟨Abynotus⟩, writes in his *Eastern History*, addressed to Philip, King of France.

{#s004-10} And where there follows, “And a prophecy is said to be current among us”, etc., this is explained from Calixtus: in the *Journey of St James* you will find where we read that at Salez Cadis there is an image of Mahomet holding keys in its hands, which ⟨will fall⟩, or falls, when … And the law of Mahomet is called *salem* in the Arabic tongue, while *cadis* is “mountain”.

{#s004-11} Secondly, note that there is a prophecy among the Saracens that their sect must come to an end; and the reason is that, according to the astrologers, it began under such a sign that it is not to last long. And this they themselves grant: for it is to end by the year 1500. And if it should happen to last longer, this will be rather beyond nature, and on account of our sins.

{#s004-12} The law of the Hebrews, however, will last until the end of the world; for they must be in the world as a witness to the death of Christ, as Paul wrote to the Romans, saying: “The remnant of Israel shall be converted in the end.” To the argument it is said that Sacred Scripture existed under the law [of nature], and if not explicitly, then implicitly.
