{#s009-01} The second question of the first distinction, where it is asked whether the ultimate end has only one aspect of enjoyability. It seems so, because, [according to] *Ethics* I, just as being and truth are in every genus, so is the good; therefore, if [each] has its own goodness, [it has] its own enjoyability too; and consequently, since in God there is one [aspect] and another, there will be another aspect of enjoyability. To the contrary: the ultimate end corresponds to the first efficient cause; but there is only one first efficient cause, and under one aspect; therefore there is only one end.

{#s009-02} [[p. 19|r° · Di.i. Q.ii · sig. b.2]] The Doctor divides this question into four articles. In the first article of the second, which begins “On the second article it is said that it is not possible, speaking of the absolute power of God, that any one of the blessed should enjoy the divine essence without enjoying a person”, etc.: this is the opinion of Henry, *Quodlibet* II, q. 7. When he comes to solving the principal arguments, and solving the second, he says thus: “To the second it must be said that those things which regard essence and person uniformly”, etc.: so Henry replies, *Quodlibet* I, q. 1. But according to the Doctor’s view we shall consider four things: first, the enjoyment of the wayfarer as to its possibility; second, the enjoyment of the blessed, speaking of the absolute power of God; third, the enjoyment of the blessed, speaking of the power of the creature; fourth, the power and the enjoyment of wayfarer and blessed in fact.

{#s009-03} As to the first, he says that it is possible for a wayfarer to enjoy the essence without enjoying a person, and this with ordered enjoyment, since the essence is something absolute and prior. As to the second, that by the absolute power of God he sees no contradiction in its being possible, on the side of intellect and of will, that the act of each should terminate at the divine essence or at a person, or at one person and not another: for instance, that the intellect should see the essence and not a person, or one person and not another; and that the will should enjoy the essence and not a person, or one person and not another. To the third, that the intellect cannot by its natural power see the essence and not the persons, since the object manifests three persons and the intellect is moved to them naturally; nor can the will, when they are presented, fail to enjoy them, either absolutely or in an ordered way. And the reason is that, although it is in the power of the will that an act be posited in being or not posited, yet it is not in its power that the act, once posited, should have that condition or freedom; and note this carefully. To the fourth, he says that in fact there is one enjoyment and vision of the essence in three persons, or of the three persons in the essence.

{#s009-04} But does whoever sees the essence see all creatures? To which some say no, because [otherwise] the revelations of saints and angels would perish. Therefore, if the blessed person, seeing the essence, does not see all creatures, or all things represented there, in what way do they have new knowledge? It is said by a certain [doctor] that by the same act God is known and the things known in God are known. But that new knowledge comes about through new relations to secondary objects; for acts have a necessary and essential relation to the primary object, but not to the others, namely the secondary objects, because there can be [[p. 20|v° · Di.i. Q.iii]] an act without such a relation. It is said, therefore, that by the accession of a new relation of that act to a secondary object, the secondary object, which previously was not understood, comes to be understood; and this by the prior act to which the relation accrues; and that relation is extrinsically supervenient. But when the divine essence is known, are the ideas known? It is said, no; but if they are known, knowledge of them is had through new relations, as has been said.

{#s009-05} But does it belong to the essence to beatify except as in three [persons]? It is said that it does, because otherwise the Father would not be blessed without the Son. And therefore it is said that the divine essence, under its absolute aspect, is the beatific object, and not as in three, although it is always in three.

{#s009-06} Note, in the first conclusion, where the authority of Augustine, *On the Trinity* VII, c. 2, is cited: “If the essence is said relatively, it is not essence”, because every essence that is said relatively is something apart from the relative [term], that is, it is absolute. And this holds true of the first relation. But if a relation is founded upon a relation, as if likeness were founded upon proportion, Augustine’s rule will not hold of the second relation; for when that relation is removed, what remains is not absolute but relative.

{#s009-07} To the argument at the beginning I say that there is one ultimate end in itself, yet it has certain distinct aspects which are not formally aspects of the ultimate end; and so it is possible to enjoy it under the aspect of ultimate end without enjoying it under those aspects.
