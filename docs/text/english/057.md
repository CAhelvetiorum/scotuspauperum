{#s057-h .heading} [Second question.]

{#s057-01} The second question of the nineteenth distinction, where it is asked whether one person is in another by circumincession. It seems not, since there would be confusion, and it would be against that saying of Athanasius, ‘neither confounding the persons.’ To the contrary, John 14: ‘I am in the Father and the Father is in me’; and the Master adduces authorities of Augustine.

{#s057-02} In the first article is set down the opinion of Henry in the *Summa*, that one thing is in another in two ways: either according to its whole self or according to a part. And in the ⟨argument⟩ of the distinction is set down the opinion of Giles, who says that power pertains to omnipotence in the Father, not in the Son. And a little before the solution of the principal arguments, where he says, ‘And from this appears the unreasonableness of that saying,’ etc.: here Brother William of Ware is touched upon, in the present distinction, the single question, third opinion.

{#s057-03} But about this question something is certain and something doubtful. For it is certain that one person is in another, as is clear from the authority of the Saviour, John 14: ‘I am in the Father and the Father is in me’; and from Augustine in the book *On Faith, to Peter*; and from Hilary, *On the Trinity* 3; and from Ambrose on the Second Epistle to the Corinthians, as the Master alleges in the text. But there is doubt about the manner in which one person is in another. And about this there are several opinions. One is Henry's, where cited above; and he says that one person is not in another according to its whole self, though it certainly is according to a part, namely according to the essence, for the essence is something of each. The Doctor refutes this.

{#s057-04} For the opinion of the Doctor is that one person is in another. For the evidence of which it must be noted that something belongs to something by reason of a part, as the Philosopher holds, *Physics* 5: that a man is healed because his heart or chest is healed. In a second way, something belongs to some whole in such a way that it does not belong precisely to any part of it, as for a man to be risible; and this is true in all heterogeneous bodies, that is, bodies of unlike parts. In a third way, something belongs to something common in such a way that it belongs to each of its parts, as is clear of fire and [[p. 92|v° · Di.xix. Q.ii]]homogeneous bodies: for the whole fire is hot, and each part of it is hot.

{#s057-05} But before I come to the point, I set down four negative conclusions, then an affirmative one. The first conclusion: that this circumincession is not formally through the unity of the divine essence, in such a way that one person is in another through the essence which is in this one and which is a part of it, as a bird is in a snare by its foot. And the reason is that in the Father is the whole Son, and the whole Father in the Word, according to Ambrose. The second conclusion: that this circumincession is not formally through the identity of the persons, since they have no identity except in the essence; and circumincession is not posited among essential things, although they are the same in it. The third conclusion: that this circumincession is not formally through the intimacy of the divine persons, since the essential things are more intimate to one another than the persons, yet circumincession is not posited in them. The fourth conclusion: that this circumincession is not formally through the mutual origin of the divine persons, since, granted that the Holy Spirit did not proceed from the Son, there would be no less circumincession of one in the other.

{#s057-06} The fifth conclusion, affirmative as to the matter in hand: that one person is in another, not as a nature in a suppositum, nor as form in matter, but as a subsistent in a subsistent really distinct, by a mutual assistance of presence. Now to subsist is the same as to be through itself incommunicably. It belongs to the person first, just as to act in creatures, or to be produced, belongs first to the whole suppositum. This is proved by Ambrose in the hymn: ‘the whole Father in the Word, and the whole Son in the Father.’ Further, Augustine in the book *On Faith, to Peter* says: ‘Because of the natural unity, the whole Father is in the Son and in the Holy Spirit; the Holy Spirit too is in the Father and the Son; and none of them is outside any of them, because of the unity of the divine essence.’

{#s057-07} And the Doctor says that the ground of this in-being is the essence and the relation together: since, just as the distinction of a person does not of itself found likeness, so the essence together with the relation is the ground of this in-existence or in-assistance, although the essence is the principal ground. But [if it be asked whether] this mode of being-in could be reduced to one of those modes of Aristotle, it is said not: for in those there is some distinction in nature, as can plainly be seen in *Physics* 4; but here there is no distinction in nature.

{#s057-08} But a certain [author] explains how one person is in another through the essence and the property: for a subsistent is in another subsistent by presence, as if two bodies which were essentially the same were in the same place; so it seems that one person is in another, since by divine power this can [[p. 93|r° · Di.xx. Q.i]]be done. To the argument at the beginning, I say that if each person were in the other as its part, there would be confusion; but the persons are not so in one another.
