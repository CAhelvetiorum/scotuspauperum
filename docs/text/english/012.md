{#s012-h .heading} [Fifth question.]

{#s012-01} [[p. 23|r° · Di.i. Q.v]] The fifth and last question of the first distinction, where the Doctor asks five questions. First, whether it befits God to enjoy. Second, whether the wayfarer enjoys. Third, whether the sinner enjoys. Fourth, whether brute animals enjoy. Fifth, whether all things enjoy. It seems not [that it befits God to enjoy], because enjoyment is with respect to an end, but God has no end; therefore etc. To the contrary: God loves himself and not for the sake of anything else; therefore he enjoys himself. The consequence holds, because if he loves himself, [he does so] either by using or by enjoying.

{#s012-02} In deciding the question, it is found in some books that the Wise Man is cited for a definition of God, saying: “God is an intelligible sphere whose centre is everywhere and whose circumference is nowhere.” According to the Irrefragable Doctor, whom they call Brother Alexander of Hales, in the first part of his *Summa*, this is from Hermes Trismegistus. In his *[De] proprietatibus*, Brother Bartholomew asserts that it is from Secundus the Philosopher, in [the book] *On the Life of the Philosophers*.

{#s012-03} But coming to the question. To the first it is said that it befits God to enjoy, because his will enjoys its object, although it does not enjoy it as an end, for then he would be his own end. To the second question it is said that the wayfarer enjoys, because by an act of friendship he wills that it be well with God in himself, and therefore he enjoys him; for such an act is enjoyment and not an act of desire. To the third question it is said that the sinner enjoys; for although the sinner rests in something else, he does not rest immovably. To rest immovably is [to rest] in an unrestrainable act of appetite for an object that satisfies [it], that is, in God or in the infinite. To the fourth question it is said that brute animals do not enjoy. And if Augustine seems to say the contrary, his authority must be understood of enjoyment in an improper sense; for properly *to enjoy* is to cleave by love, which is not found in beasts, as can be gathered from *Ethics* VIII. To the fifth question it is said that enjoying does not befit all things; for enjoying befits those that have reason and appetite, and appetite must follow upon the act of knowing; but these do not befit stones and other inanimate things; it therefore follows that not all things enjoy. To the argument at the beginning it is said that, although God is not his own end, with respect to his will he is that object to which the character of end is naturally apt to belong.
