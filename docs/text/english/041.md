{#s041-h .heading} [Second question.]

{#s041-01} The second question is whether, if the Holy Spirit did not proceed from the Son, his real distinction from the Son could stand; that is, whether he would be distinguished from him. It seems not: for according to Boethius, *On the Trinity*, “the essence holds the unity, relation multiplies the Trinity”; therefore nothing is distinguished except what is related. To the contrary: [Augustine,] *On the Trinity* 5, chapter 17, it appears that the Holy Spirit is not the Son, even if he goes forth from the Father, because he will [go forth] not as one born but as one given. Immediately he sets out the opinion of those who say that there is no question, because it includes incompossibles; and this is Godfrey, *Quodlibet* 7, question 4. After this way [of speaking] has been attacked, there is set out the opinion of those who say that they would not be distinguished; and this is Thomas, First Part of the *Summa*, question 35, article 2, and Giles of Rome, *Quodlibet* 1, question 6.

{#s041-02} To the question some say that the question is null because [it is] impossible. But the Doctor says that about many [[p. 69|r° · Di.xii. Q.i]] impossibilities a question is raised, as about the void (*Physics* 4); and so here the question is not about impossibility, but, granted such a conditional, that is: if he did not proceed, whether he would be distinguished. But in answering the point, the Doctor says that if the Holy Spirit did not proceed from the Son, he would be distinguished from him. And the reason is that the Son is Son by his formal notion, that is, by filiation, and is distinguished from anything that is not such. But spiration, if it comes to the Son, comes to the Son already posited, and is as it were a property coming to him after his being; and so, if he [the Spirit] never proceeded from him, he would be distinguished from him. And this is confirmed by Augustine, *On the Trinity* 5, chapter 27: it appears that the Holy Spirit is not [the Son], even if he goes forth from the Father. And this is the Doctor's intention.

{#s041-03} Others say that this question is to be understood thus: if the Holy Spirit proceeded neither from the Father nor from the Son, he would not be distinguished from them. And they assign the reason by way of [the topic] “from the part in mode”, thus: if the Holy Spirit exists, he is distinct; and if he does not exist, he is not distinct; [and] “from the whole in mode”, destructively. And so they restrict [the thesis]: if the Holy Spirit did not proceed from one of the persons and [yet] proceeded, he would be distinguished; but if he proceeded from none, he would not be distinguished. To the argument at the beginning it is said that the Holy Spirit would have a relation of distinction to the Son, even if he did not proceed from him: not formally but fundamentally.
