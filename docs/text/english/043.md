{#s043-h .heading} [Second question.]

{#s043-01} [[p. 72|v° · Di.xii. Q.ii.et.iii]] The second question of the twelfth distinction, where it is asked whether the Father and the Son can be called two spirating or two spirators. It seems that they are two spirators: for just as we grant that they are two spirating, so in like manner also spirators, since both derive from the verb. To the contrary: if they were two spirators, they would be two substances, which is heretical.

{#s043-02} Going further, he asks why a numeral term is allowed to be added to adjectival terms, so that they are called “two spirating.” And the opinion of Thomas is set out, First Part of the *Summa*, question 35, last article, in solving the last argument: he says that they are two spirating on account of the plurality of supposits, but not two spirators, on account of the one spiration.

{#s043-03} But coming to the Doctor’s intention, it is said that they can be called two spirating but not two spirators. And he gives the reason: no verbal noun signifies such a principle of acting except as it is apt to denominate a supposit adjectivally. For just as *reading* (*lectio*) signifies the act quidditatively, by way of a habit and of rest, and the denominative of the supposit adjectivally, so *spirator* signifies the power of spirating as it connotes the supposit. And since there is one power in the Father and the Son, and a numeral term attached to anything determinable places its significate about that thing, it does not seem that one should grant, by the force of the words, that they are two spirators; for the spirative power would seem to be numbered in them, which is false. Others say that a verbal noun signifies by way of a habit and of rest, that is, it in some way signifies a substance; and so “two spirators” means two substances. Two substances have two powers; but in the Father and the Son there is only one power of spirating; therefore they cannot be called two spirators. But it is not so with the participle, since it is an adjective following the verb and is put in place of the verb. And since the Father and the Son have one being in spirating, that is, one power, they are called two spirating, that is, two having one power of spirating. And this is the intention of a certain new doctor. To the argument at the beginning I say that the case is not alike, from what has already been said in the question.
