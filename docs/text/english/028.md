{#s028-h .heading} [Second question.]

{#s028-01} The second question of the fourth distinction, where it is asked whether this is true: “God is Father, Son and Holy Spirit.” For it seems not to be true, because its contradictory seems to be true, namely “No God is Father and Son and Holy Spirit,” since every singular is true, etc. To the contrary: Father and Son and Holy Spirit are one God, according to Augustine, *On the Trinity* VIII.

{#s028-02} In this question there are no opinions. But coming to it, he answers in the affirmative, and this is confirmed by Augustine, *On the Trinity* VIII, in many places. The reason is this: ‘God’ signifies the divine nature as apt to be predicated of supposits, and that significate is the same for the three persons. Hence ‘God’, put in the subject, conveys the divine nature with a co-reference to a supposit; while ‘Father, Son and Holy Spirit’ on the side of the predicate convey the same nature and name supposits; and so the proposition is true. It is true that its converse, namely “the Father is God, the Son is God, the Holy Spirit is God,” is more a formal predication than the former, because the former looks like an inverted predication of a common term of its supposits. But this, “the Father is deity, the Son is deity,” is the same by identity: [[p. 46|v° · Di.v. Q.i]] the first, “the Son is God,” is formal; in the abstract, “the Son is deity” is a predication by identity and not formal.

{#s028-03} Further, he says that “God is Father, Son and Holy Spirit” is true inasmuch as ‘God’ stands for this God insofar as he is a being *per se* by deity, but not for some supposit properly so called in which the divine nature is, that is, for Father, Son and Holy Spirit. And note, roughly in the middle of the question, concerning the truth of this proposition, “Colour disperses [sight]”: for what does ‘colour’ stand? The Doctor says that it stands for its significate, namely for existing colour, but not for some colour inferior to this colour, for instance for this colour on this surface or on that one. The reason is that these factors contracting colour are not the cause of the truth of this proposition. To the argument at the beginning I say that that is not precisely its contradictory if the distribution is made over the persons; rather a fallacy is committed.
