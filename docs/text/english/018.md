{#s018-h .heading} [Third question.]

{#s018-01} The third question of the third distinction, which asks whether some other transcendental is posited as the first object of our intellect, having a commonness equal to being; that is, whether the true is the object of our intellect. It seems so: the true is the object of the intellect, therefore not being. The consequence holds because there are not two adequate objects of one power. The antecedent is clear from Anselm, *On Truth*, who says: “Truth is rectitude perceptible by the mind alone.” To the contrary: “Being and thing are impressed first on the soul,” [[p. 33|r° · Di.iii. Q.iiii]] according to Avicenna; therefore not the true.

{#s018-02} In this question the opinion of Giles of Rome is set out, who says that the true is the object of our intellect; it is set down in *Quodlibet* IV q. 10. But the Doctor refutes it, and the reason for the refutation is this. He calls the formal aspect of an object that according to which the object moves the power, as the formal aspect of an agent, or of acting, is called the form according to which the agent acts. But such an aspect of the object cannot be a relation, as truth is; for the object must be absolute, but truth implies a relation to the power. This is confirmed by Aristotle, *On the Soul* II: he does not say that the visible as such is the object [of sight], because it implies a relation to the power, but that colour is, and this is something absolute. So in the present case. Again, the object must be first; but what is relative is not first, because it presupposes something absolute; therefore etc. But how does the true stand? I say that the true is a condition consequent upon being; yet not such that it is posited *per se* as the object.

{#s018-03} But suppose someone were to ask: if being in its most common notion is the object of the intellect and moves the intellect, why can whatever is contained under being not move the intellect naturally? Then we should conclude that God could be known naturally in this way. I say to this that the first object of a power is assigned as that which is adequate to the power by reason of the power [itself], not what is adequate to the power in some state. Just so, the first adequate object of sight is not taken to be precisely what is adequate to sight existing in a medium lit by a candle, but what is of a nature to be adequate to sight of itself and from its own nature. So in the present case I say that being is the object, and not under the aspect of infinity. Or I say that God can be known naturally under the aspect of being, but not as such a being, that is, as the being of God.

{#s018-04} To the argument at the beginning it is said that the authority does not prove that the true is the adequate object, but that it is apprehended only by the intellect.
