{#s065-h .heading} [Second question.]

{#s065-01} The second question of the twenty-sixth distinction, where it is asked whether relation in a creature differs really from its foundation. It seems not: Augustine, ‘everything that is [said] with respect to another is something apart from that by which it is said relatively’. To the contrary, through Augustine: because if [the relation were] something other than that [foundation, while being the same], it would differ from itself.

{#s065-02} To the question it is said that it does.

{#s065-03} But on this, for the sake of greater clarification, we shall consider four things. First, whether in creatures relation is a real being or a being of reason. Second, I shall speak to the point of the question, namely whether the relation of a creature is the same as its foundation. Third, by what it is efficiently caused, [that is,] relation in creatures. Fourth, in what manner relation is caused in creatures.

{#s065-04} [[p. 103|r° · Di.xxvi. Q.ii]]As to the first article, it is said that relation in creatures is a real being. And the reason is that it can be apprehended by sense; for sight can judge that this whiteness is like that one, [and] likeness is a relation. This is against Aureolus, who says that relation is a being of reason.

{#s065-05} As to the second, whether relation in a creature differs really from its foundation: it is the opinion of Henry that it does not, because it comes to a thing without a change in it. It is refuted. It is the opinion of the Doctor that relation differs really from its foundation; for things which are so related that one can be produced without the other are really distinct; but relation is such. As, if God were to create one single whiteness, it would be like none; and so [there would be] the foundation without the relation, at least the formal one. From this I lay down some conclusions. The first: that it is not impossible for God to separate the relation from the foundation, the existence of the same [foundation remaining], especially in the case of those relations in which there is no such dependence on the foundation; since God can supply the place of the terminating [extreme] without imperfection. For with the others, that is, with the dependence of the creature on God, it is otherwise; for in these, if God supplied the place of the dependence, it would be [a mark] of imperfection, which is not to be attributed to God.

{#s065-06} The second conclusion: that it is possible for relations actually to be separated from their foundation. This conclusion is clear, because things which are essentially distinct are mutually separable, as relation and foundation are related. That they are essentially distinct is clear, because the foundation belongs to one category and the relation to another. The third conclusion is that a relation can be in act without its extremes. This too is clear, because they are essentially distinct, and so one can be without the other. The fourth conclusion: that the extremes of a relation can actually be without the relation. This is clear, because they are absolute and prior to the relation; and so God can make [them] without the relation, as two white things without likeness — I mean formal [likeness].

{#s065-07} The fifth conclusion: that one correlative can actually exist without the other. And this is clear, because they are essentially distinct. And if you said, ‘they have a mutual dependence, and so one will not be separated from the other’, against this: one does not depend on the other as on an intrinsic cause, but [as on] an extrinsic one; but God can supply extrinsic causality; therefore, etc. And so God could supply the term of paternity and filiation, though not the filiation according to that *ratio* by which one is formally called son, since that would be [a mark] of imperfection. But [if] he supplied the place of the terminating, [then] by the first [way] it would follow that he had a new being, which is impossible; by the second, however, not.

{#s065-08} [[p. 104|v° · Di.xxvii. Q.i]]As to the third article, namely by what relation is efficiently caused: some say that it is caused by that which generates the foundation of the relation, as my likeness [is caused] by that which generates my whiteness. It is refuted. There is another way: that it is generated by the opposite foundation in which it is terminated, as my likeness is efficiently from that which generates your whiteness, in which my likeness is terminated. And this opinion the followers of Scotus commonly hold.

{#s065-09} As to the fourth article, in what manner relation is caused in creatures, four conclusions are laid down. The first: that relation in creatures can be caused through action and passion, for through these some form is impressed. The second conclusion: that relation can be caused successively; this is clear from the Philosopher, *Physics* 5, for relation can be caused through motion. The third conclusion: that relation is caused through change; this is clear, for if it is caused through motion, then through change. The fourth conclusion: that relation, according to some, can be caused by degrees; for it can be intensified and remitted, and [a thing] can become more like and less like through the reception or withdrawal of degrees.

{#s065-10} To the argument at the beginning, the answer is clear from what has been said.
