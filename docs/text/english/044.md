{#s044-h .heading} [Third question.]

{#s044-01} The third question of the twelfth distinction, where it is asked whether the Father and the Son spirate the Holy Spirit uniformly. It seems not: Augustine, *On the Trinity* 15, chapter 7: the Holy Spirit proceeds principally from the Father; therefore not principally from the Son; therefore not altogether uniformly. To the contrary: [[p. 73|r° · Di.xii. Q.iiii]] they spirate the Holy Spirit by one principle, inasmuch as they are altogether one, from the preceding question; therefore, etc.

{#s044-02} While he speaks in solving the principal arguments: a certain doctor nevertheless says that the Father spirates more principally, while the Son spirates only principally, and that they therefore do not spirate uniformly. This is Brother Alexander of Alessandria, in the present distinction, question 2, and Saint Bonaventure, question 2, at the end, and in the third literal doubt, when he adds. Another doctor, however, contradicts him: this is Brother William ⟨Varro⟩, in the present distinction.

{#s044-03} But coming to the question, he says that they spirate the Holy Spirit by one principle, inasmuch as they are altogether one, from the preceding question; therefore altogether uniformly. But for a fuller explanation he says that the act can be considered in three ways: in itself, or as it is of the term, or as it is compared to the acting supposits. In the first two ways there is a complete uniformity, or rather unity, since there is one action and one term produced. Speaking in the third way: just as the spirative power itself is communicated to the Son by the Father, so too the Son has from the Father that he spirates; and thus the Father spirates from himself, but the Son not from himself. And if it is said, “Therefore the Father spirates the Holy Spirit first,” the answer is no. Hence it is not to be understood that the Father spirates before the Son, as he generates prior in origin to spirating; but the order is this. First, there is in the Father each fecundity, from himself. Second, there is in the Father the act of the first fecundity, and yet in the Son there is the second fecundity. Third, there is the act of the second fecundity, at once from the Father and the Son, who then have that fecundity, yet still with a certain order, since that act is the Father’s from himself, but the Son’s not from himself but from the Father; just as in the second sign that fecundity is not the Son’s from himself, but is the Father’s from himself. There is therefore no order of origin between the spiration of the Father and of the Son, as though the Father spirated in some sign of origin in which the Son did not spirate; rather, in the same sign of origin the Father and the Son spirate together. There is, however, an order of those spirating, in spirating, since in that second sign of origin the Father spirates from himself, but the Son not from himself.

{#s044-04} Note that in this question of Scotus you have the signs; and more fully in the first question of the second book. To the argument at the beginning I say that the authorities of Augustine are to be understood thus: that if the Son spirates, he has this from the Father; not that the Father spirates first, but that the Father has it from himself, the Son from the Father.
