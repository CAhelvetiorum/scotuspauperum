{#s040-h .heading} [Eleventh distinction.]

{#s040-01} On the eleventh distinction it is asked whether the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son. It seems not: for Damascene, chapter 7, says: “We say that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and rests in the Son.” To the contrary, in the Nicene Creed: “who proceeds from the Father and the Son”; and Athanasius in his Creed, etc. Having proved that it is so, he adds [that] others prove this order of production; and this is Henry, *Quodlibet* 6, question 1.

{#s040-02} But coming to the question, it is said that it is so. And for greater clarity four things will be set out. First, the point of the question. Second, whether the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father through the Son. Third, what is the productive principle of the Holy Spirit. Fourth, what is the formal term of active spiration.

{#s040-03} To the first it is said that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son, although the Greeks say the opposite. And they prove it by the saying of Andrew, when he was preaching in Greece, who said that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and abides in the Son. But the Latins say that he proceeds from both. And they cite authorities. The first is of the Lord and Saviour in the Gospel: “If I ascend into heaven, I will send you the Paraclete.” Again: “When the Holy Spirit is come, whom I will send,” etc. Again, John 16: “He shall glorify me, for he shall receive of mine.” But to receive from him is to proceed from him. Again, John 20: when the Lord willed to give the Holy Spirit, he breathed on them; [and] the breathing is a proceeding from him. Again, among the Greeks, Athanasius, a Greek; second, Dionysius, a Greek; third, Chrysostom, a Greek, say that the Holy Spirit proceeds from both. Again, [the title] *On the Highest Trinity* in the *Extra*, chapter 1, where it is said that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son.

{#s040-04} As to the second, I set out four conclusions. The first is that the Holy Spirit is not from the Father through the Son in such a way that the Son is the principle *by which* of the Holy Spirit. For otherwise the Son would be formally in the Father, as heat is in fire; and heat stands in heating as that by which. But if the Son were formally in the Father, the Father could be the Son and conversely, which is false. The second conclusion is that the Holy Spirit is not from the Father through the Son as through an instrument; for then the Son would be less perfect than the Father, since an instrument is less perfect than that whose [instrument] it is, that is, than the cause. The third conclusion is [[p. 68|v° · Di.xi. Q.ii]] that the Holy Spirit is not from the Father through the Son as through a secondary cause. For it would follow that he would not be equally from the Father and the Son, which is false; for they produce equally, and by the same productive power existing in the two, and not by different ones. The fourth conclusion is that the Father produces the Holy Spirit through the Son; but he is understood to produce through another because he communicated to him the same productive power.

{#s040-05} As to the third, what the productive principle of the Holy Spirit is: it is said, the fruitful will. This is proved by Augustine, *On the Trinity* 9, where he says that, just as fruitful memory is the principle of the Son himself, so fruitful will is the principle of the Holy Spirit.

{#s040-06} As to the fourth, what the formal term of spiration is: it is said, the whole Holy Spirit. For the term is that which is acquired through spiration; but what is acquired is the Holy Spirit; therefore etc. But [it is asked whether] active spiration is constitutive of a person, that is, whether the Father and the Son are constituted by active spiration. Auriol says that active spiration concurs in the constitution of the Word together with the essence and filiation, and in the constitution of the person of the Father together with the essence and paternity. But the Doctor's opinion is that active spiration is not constitutive of the Word or of the Father, since the whole intellectual fruitfulness is understood prior to the fruitful volition; and thus the persons are understood as already constituted. To the argument at the beginning it is said that the authority is understood of the will and not of the Holy Spirit; for the will, which is the principle of spirating, is itself from the Father into the Son.
