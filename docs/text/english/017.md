{#s017-h .heading} [Second question.]

{#s017-01} The second question of the third distinction, which asks whether God is the natural adequate object of the wayfarer’s intellect. It seems so: God is the first, that is, the most perfect, of all knowables; and the first in each genus is the cause of being for the others, from *Metaphysics* II, as the first hot thing is the cause of heat in all others; therefore God is the reason for knowing all objects. To the contrary: the first object by primacy of adequacy receives the predication of the objects contained under it; God is not such; therefore etc.

{#s017-02} At the beginning he says: in this question there is one opinion, the opinion of Thomas in the First [[p. 32|v° · Di.iii. Q.iii]] Part of the *Summa*, q. 12, a. 4, and q. 85, a. 1. And he concludes that [it is] not [so]. Having refuted that, there is another opinion there, which holds God to be the first object of the intellect; it is Henry’s, *Quodlibet* XV q. 9 and *Quodlibet* XIII q. 9.

{#s017-03} But to come to the Doctor’s intention: this question takes its origin from the preceding one, because there it was raised and not determined what the first object of the intellect by primacy of adequacy is. To which it is said that the first object by primacy of adequacy is being *qua* being. For it alone has these primacies, namely of commonness and of virtuality. But to maintain this he posits and presupposes that being is common, univocal to God and creature, to substance and accident. Again, one must understand that being is common to all in such a way that it is included quidditatively in all, except in the first differences (namely of being), the last (namely of individuals), and in the properties. For in the intermediate differences, that is, those taken from distinct realities, being is indeed included quidditatively: for instance in *rational*. For *animal* is taken from the sensitive soul and *rational* from the intellective soul, which are true realities; and each of them is being and includes being.

{#s017-04} But how is that metaphysical saying to be understood, “There is nothing that escapes the notion of being”? It is said that it is to be understood [as being] quidditatively or denominatively. The first and last differences and the properties are being denominatively; everything else is being and includes being quidditatively. And according to the Doctor, God is being, and [it is predicated] quidditatively; according to Francis, denominatively, though not by accidental denomination.

{#s017-05} Note, before the second article, where he sets down that [rule] of *Posterior Analytics* I, that *per se* propositions do not convert. He says that if a predicate is said of a subject *per se*, the subject will not in the same way be said of the predicate *per se*, but *per accidens*. Therefore, if *being is one* is *per se* in the second mode, *one is being* is not *per se* in the second mode but as it were *per accidens*; and so too *the risible is man*.

{#s017-06} To the argument at the beginning it is said that God is the reason for knowing under the aspect of goodness, but not under the aspect of being.
