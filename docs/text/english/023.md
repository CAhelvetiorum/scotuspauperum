{#s023-h .heading} [Eighth question.]

{#s023-01} The eighth question of the third distinction, which asks whether there is begotten knowledge in our intellect. It seems not: the Philosopher proves in *Metaphysics* VII that there is no generation of the form alone, but of the composite; but knowledge implies not a composite but a form alone; therefore it is not generated. To the contrary, Augustine, *On the Trinity* IX, ch. 12: knowledge is brought forth by the knower and the known.

{#s023-02} In this question there are no opinions, but in replying he says that there is. He then asks to what kind of entity intellection belongs, and replies briefly with three conclusions. The first: it is not a substance. Proof: a substance is not present and absent [while its subject remains], but intellection is. The second conclusion: it is not a generation, for generation belongs to the composite (*Metaphysics* VII), but intellection is not a composite; and so he concludes that it is not properly a generation. The third conclusion: understanding and willing in us are neither actions nor passions; whereas saying, that is, understanding, producing, and spiriting, that is, eliciting love, are actions [[p. 39|r° · Di.iii. Q.ix]] in us, and in the genus of action or quasi in the genus of action. But a certain [doctor] says, and more clearly, that understanding is an action not in the genus of action but in the genus of quality. The reason is that Aristotle, *Ethics* VI, calls understanding and willing immanent actions. But this will be discussed more fully in the next question. To the argument at the beginning it is said that the ultimate [term of] generation is the composite, but the immediate [term] is the form itself.
