{#s058-h .heading} [Twentieth distinction.]

{#s058-01} On the twentieth distinction it is asked whether the three persons are equal in power. It seems not, since the Son, as he himself testifies, can do nothing of himself. To the contrary, by the testimony of John: whatever the Father can do, the Son likewise. To the question it is said that they are; and this is shown by Augustine in the book *On Faith, to Peter*: for Augustine, speaking of the three persons, says, ‘None of them,’ he says, ‘precedes another in eternity or surpasses him in power.’ And therefore three things will be seen about this. First, a distinction of power will be set down. Second, it will be seen whether the persons are equal in power. Third, to what that power in which they are equal extends.

{#s058-02} As to the first article, the Philosopher says, *Metaphysics* 9, that power (*potentia*) is taken in many ways. For first there is mathematical potency, as a point is in potency to a line. Second, there is logical potency, namely the non-repugnance of terms. Third, there is the potency which is a difference of being, as the Philosopher holds, *Physics* 3, that being is divided by act and potency. And these three modes are not to the purpose. In a fourth way potency is taken for a principle, and this in two ways: in one way it is taken for a passive principle, and this potency is not to be posited in God, since it signifies imperfection; in the second way potency is taken for an active principle, and it is of this power that we speak here.

{#s058-03} As to the second article, it is said, first, that in God there is active power; second, that in divine things the persons are equal in this power. The first is clear: for active power signifies perfection, and every perfection is to be attributed to God; and this can be had from the Commentator, *Metaphysics* 8. The second is clear from Augustine, *Against Maximinus* book 3, as the Master also adduces in the text, first by the authority of the Saviour: ‘All things,’ he says, ‘that are my Father's are mine’ — which would not be true unless they were equal in power. This conclusion can be confirmed thus. The Father, in begetting the Son, communicates to the Son whatever he has except the relations of origin; but the Father has power, which does not signify a relation of origin, since he communicates it to the Son, etc. Again: just as the Father communicates wisdom to the Son, so he communicates power to him, since each signifies an attributal perfection; but the Father communicates all wisdom to the Son; therefore he communicates all power to him.

{#s058-04} [[p. 94|v° · Di.xxi. Q.i]]As to the third article, it is said that the correlative of active power is the possible. Now the possible is said in two ways: in one way as it is opposed to the impossible, in another as it is opposed to what is necessary. But the divine power does not extend to the possible in the first way, since in this way it is possible for God to be, and yet this is not the term of any active power. Therefore this active power must regard the possible in the second way, as it is opposed to what is necessary of itself. But whatever is in God is necessary of itself, and whatever is outside God is possible to be. Therefore the active power in which the divine persons are equal extends to every possible being, and so to everything that is outside God.

{#s058-05} But [it is objected that] the power of begetting falls under omnipotence. To this Giles says, book 1, distinction 20, question 2, that the power of begetting falls under the omnipotence of the Father, and not under the omnipotence of the Son. But this opinion is false. Therefore I say that the power of begetting does not fall under omnipotence, which is proved thus: according to the saints the omnipotence in the Father and the Son is numerically the same; but in the Son there is no power of begetting; therefore the power of begetting does not fall under omnipotence.

{#s058-06} To the argument at the beginning, it is said that it is understood [to mean] that he cannot do anything that is against the will of the Father.
