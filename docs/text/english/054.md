{#s054-h .heading} [Second question.]

{#s054-01} The second question of the eighteenth distinction, where it is asked whether that positive [reality] of the pre-existing charity which remains in the increased [charity] is the whole essence of the intense charity, so that, if intense charity were posited without a subject, it would have in itself essentially no positive reality other than that which was there before in the remiss charity. It seems so, on the authority of the Philosopher, *Metaphysics* 8: ‘Forms are like numbers.’ To the contrary: then the charity of any blessed one whatever would be equal, in the nature of charity, to the charity of Christ, which is false.

{#s054-02} At the beginning of the solution is set down the opinion of Thomas, *Prima secundae*, question 52, articles 1 and 2, who says that the increase of charity comes about according to its being in the subject, but rather through a greater disposition of the subject or a greater removal of impediment, not according to charity in itself. To which [belongs] the argument from disposition, where the Doctor [[p. 87|r° · Di.xviii. Q.ii]]says: ‘it is said that more and less is in different subjects’: this is the reply of Giles of Rome, *Quodlibet* 2, question 14, and *Quodlibet* 6, question 9. Next, where he says, ‘Another way also, which is from disposition or the removal of the contrary,’ etc.: this is Giles where cited above, and Henry, *Quodlibet* 14, question 3. And where he says further, ‘the third way, from the rooting of the form,’ etc.: this is Giles, question 3 and question 9.

{#s054-03} But coming to the question, it is said that it is so: that it is augmentable according to its nature. But before the determination of the question I shall set down some conclusions opposed to the aforesaid opinions. The first, negative, conclusion is this: that the extensive greatness and smallness of the subject, or of the form, or of both, is not called the cause of receiving more and less; for otherwise it would follow that a large man would have greater charity than a small man, which is false. The second conclusion: that approach to and removal from its contrary is not the cause of receiving greater or lesser charity. This is expressly clear in the blessed [in heaven]; and also because in the state of innocence grace would have been in different people according to more and less, where nevertheless there would have been no contrary. The third conclusion: that approach and recess with respect to the imposition of the name is not the cause of receiving more and less. This is clear: for nothing would be charitable unless it were in the highest degree, which is false. The fourth conclusion: that charity does not receive more and less according to the disposition in the subject. For if this were true, since at the beginning of charity the disposition is already had, then at the beginning of charity the whole charity would be present, which is false. The fifth conclusion: that rooting is not the cause of receiving more and less. This is clear: for in some old Christian it can be more rooted than in some new one; but many times we see greater charity in a new Christian than in an old. The sixth conclusion, affirmative and true: that the cause of receiving more and less is the latitude of degrees in charity itself.

{#s054-04} But for fuller clarification three things will be seen. First, whether charity, a quality, etc., are augmentable according to their quiddity. Second, supposing that they are augmentable, in what way. Third, whether they are diminishable.

{#s054-05} As to the first, it is said that charity and a quality are not augmentable according to their quiddity: for if so, it would follow that the quidditative notion would not consist in an indivisible, which nevertheless it does, as Aristotle witnesses, *Metaphysics* 8. As to the second, it is said that charity, or a form, is aug[[p. 88|v° · Di.xviii. Q.iii]]mented by a new individual degree. For either it is increased through its formal notion, or according to its specific nature, or by a new individual degree. But not through the nature nor through the formal notion. Of the formal notion it is clear: for otherwise a whiteness of four degrees and a whiteness of five degrees would not have the same formal notion, which is false. Nor according to the specific nature, since it would follow that of a thing hot in four degrees and one hot in five, one would be more in species than the other. And so one must say that it comes about by a new individual degree.

{#s054-06} As to the third, I set down four conclusions. The first, that infused charity is diminishable, is clear, since it is augmentable: for augmentation comes about when someone rightly and worthily receives the sacraments of the Church. The second conclusion: that infused charity is never in fact diminished. This is clear: for if it were in fact diminished, this would be by mortal or venial sin; not by mortal, since by that charity is wholly taken away, and so it is not diminished but taken away; nor by venial sin, since those who are in such [sin] can have charity, and not diminished, since that [sin] does not take it away. The third conclusion: that acquired charity is diminished by the cessation of its acts; and this can be had from *Ethics* 2 and 3. The fourth conclusion: that acquired charity is diminished by the frequent repetition of acts contrary to its own; this can be had from *Ethics* 2, etc.

{#s054-07} To the argument at the beginning, I say that if something were added that was a quidditative part to a pre-existing quiddity, it would change the species, as if something that is a part of number is added to a pre-existing number. But it is not so in the case at hand: for the degree of charity added to charity is not of a nature to be a quidditative part of charity.
