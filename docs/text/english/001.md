{#s001-h .heading} [[p. 1|r°]] To the most reverend father in Christ and most illustrious lord, the lord Alfonso of Aragon, most worthy Archbishop of Zaragoza: his subject Guillermus Gorris, after most devout kisses of the hands, most humbly commends himself.

{#s001-01} I am not unaware, most reverend father in Christ, born of most illustrious lineage, that I was admonished by your vicar general, at your instigation, to write something from my modest moral studies for Your Dignity. Nor have I so far forgotten your commands that, though I have long refrained from writing, I should appear disobedient to Your Dignity; rather, had I written sooner, I should not have done it with sufficient care. And what I had resolved to do I judged must be put off to another time, partly owing to the length of time it required, partly because I was without my own homeland.

{#s001-02} Since, however, the theological doctrine of Scotus seemed to many, and not without reason, subtle and obscure, not only their benefit — which nature bids us favour — but also the deep remembrance of your commands admonished me that, negligence having been put to flight, I should forbear no longer, but should come to their aid, if not in one kind of writing then at least in another, and should obey Your Excellent Dignity. For this reason I resolved that this doctrine should be drawn up, like water, out of a dark well lying close to the centre of the earth, and be clearly transferred into another little well that is not deep. And this, accomplished by divine gift in summary form, I send to you, most reverend and most illustrious prelate — and I would have you read it in such a spirit as to judge it a slight and meagre thing, scarcely worthy of being written.

{#s001-03} For, in order to comply with your commands, I wished to send you this little gift, woven, like a cheap garment, from the coarse thread of my speech — such as my gifts are wont to be. I trust indeed that Your archiepiscopal and royal Dignity, which all mortals judge to be strong, just, stern, grave, magnanimous, generous, beneficent and liberal, will brand as infamous the injuries of those who unjustly disparage it, if there be any such. Nor, moreover, will there be anything that such people can greatly find fault with, if your great zeal for study shall have corrected whatever it has found crude.

{#s001-04} But if some things in this little work should seem rather obscure to anyone, I pray that he consider that no art in letters can be grasped without an interpreter and practice. Nor is an example far to seek: can civil or canon law be learned from books alone? These, though very many, nevertheless require a teacher and a single light, who by the living voice may open the minds of his hearers; and then the hearers themselves draw from a single fountain as much as they desire.

{#s001-05} [[p. 2|v°]] For I believe that nothing can be obscure to your most excellent intellect, since men proclaim you so devoted to study that, among the youths or even the elders born of royal stock who apply themselves the more ardently to the same studies, we could set against you no one at all whom you would not appear easily to surpass. As for the virtues befitting prelates and the nobility of your lineage, you hold so eminent a position that, were I to toil at depicting your praises, I should sooner deafen the ears of my hearers with their multitude than exhaust them in words. This one thing surely remains: since in these respects we can set against you no one greater, nor even an equal, it will be right to judge you more than human.

{#s001-06} Look, then, upon this little work, wrought by lamplight in my nocturnal vigils at Toulouse. For what else I could now expend in your service I do not know: you have no need of money, you do not lack glory, you abound in understanding; by birth you excel the rest of mankind; for your inclination to learning you are praised among those who hold high office. Accept, therefore, both it and me, and command and govern at your pleasure. Given at Toulouse, on the tenth day of May, in the year of the Lord one thousand four hundred and eighty-six.
