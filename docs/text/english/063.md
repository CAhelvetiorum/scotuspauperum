{#s063-h .heading} [Twenty-fifth distinction.]

{#s063-01} On the twenty-fifth distinction it is asked whether ‘person’ in God signifies substance or relation. It seems that ‘person’ is said with respect to itself; for according to Augustine, *De Trinitate* 7, ‘person’ is said with respect to itself just as ‘God’ is. [[p. 100|v° · Di.xxv. Q.i]]To the contrary is Augustine, who says thus: the divine Trinity is of persons mutually related.

{#s063-02} To the question it is said that it signifies neither substance nor relation. And the reason is that substance is not plurified nor numbered in God; but ‘person’ is numbered and plurified; therefore it does not signify substance. This is confirmed by Augustine, *De Trinitate* 7: for the Trinity in God is of persons compared to one another.

{#s063-03} But together with this some conclusions are laid down. The first: that the *ratio* of person, as person, is not precisely absolute. This is clear, for the Father in God is said with respect to the Son, and conversely. The second conclusion: that the *ratio* of person is not precisely relative. This is clear, for someone among creatures who has neither father nor mother is a person, but not relative as such. The third conclusion: that the *ratio* of person is not at once absolute and relative. This is clear, for otherwise every person [would be both], which is false, since the Father in God is relative and not absolute.

{#s063-04} The fourth conclusion: that ‘person’ abstracts from absolute and relative, since being is divided into absolute and relative, and some things are absolute, as power, and another relative, as the Son; from which it is had that person in God is neither absolute nor relative. But against this, Thomas Anglicus says that in God there is some absolute individual. And he proves it thus: what is pointed out to the eye or with the finger is absolute; but God is pointed out in this way, for I may say ‘this God’. And so it is his opinion that in God ‘this God’ is an absolute individual.

{#s063-05} But further, in this matter the following conclusions are laid down in another way than the first ones. The first: that ‘person’ does not signify a relation of origin. The reason for which is: no relation of origin, that is, proper relation, is common to the three; but ‘person’ is a name common to the three, according to Augustine, *De Trinitate* 7, ch. 7: ‘If there are three persons,’ he says, ‘what a person is is common to them.’ Therefore ‘person’ does not signify a relation of origin, that is, a proper one. The second conclusion: that ‘person’ does not signify a common relation; which is proved thus. To whatever a lower relative is referred, to the same is the higher relative also referred, though not equally primarily. An example of this: to whatever the double is referred, namely to the half, to the same is the multiple referred, namely to the half. But the Father is called the Father of the Son; therefore, if ‘person’ signified a common relation, then the person of the Father would be called the person of the Son, which is false. The third conclusion is this: that ‘person’ does not signify quiddity, that is, substance, taking substance for essence. The reason for which is that substance or quiddity, taken for essence, is not numbered or multiplied [[p. 101|r° · Di.xxvi. Q.i]]in God; but ‘person’ is numbered and multiplied; therefore, etc. Therefore, if ‘person’ signifies neither a proper nor a common relation, nor the essence, what then does it signify? And on this, two conclusions are stated according to two opinions. For if it be held that ‘person’ signifies a negation, then ‘person’ connotes something: and primarily it is said of God, namely the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit; secondarily it connotes the relations constitutive of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit; and thirdly it connotes the essence. But if the other opinion be held, that ‘person’ signifies something positive, then it signifies neither substance nor relation, but abstracts from both. And this is the opinion of Scotus.

{#s063-06} To the argument at the beginning, the answer is clear from what is said in the text.
